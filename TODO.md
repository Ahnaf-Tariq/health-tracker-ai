# Health Tracker — Enhancement Outcome Checklist

> Implementation status: the local-only Health Tracker enhancements are complete. The optional Urdu toggle and a formal WCAG AA audit remain out of scope; no Lighthouse score or clinical-validation claim is made.

## 1. Private, accessible one-flow app and form
- [x] Build a complete, production-quality, single-purpose web app: no login, signup, login pages, user accounts, or database; single flow Landing/Form → Results plus a small Privacy/Disclaimer modal.
- [x] Make it mobile-first, responsive and readable, with keyboard-friendly controls, proper labels, dark mode, soft green/teal, readable numbers, status conveyed by icon/text as well as color, a smooth analyzing state, larger body/helper text, stronger contrast and larger control targets for older adults.
- [ ] Formally audit WCAG AA conformance; no formal audit has been performed and no compliance claim is made.
- [x] Process health data in memory only; do not store health data on the server or log it. No analytics on health fields, no third-party trackers, cookie-free by default. The user approved local rules-only analysis; no health inputs or notes are sent to a third-party LLM or other analysis service. Any optional “Save my last result on this device” uses localStorage only and is off by default.
- [x] Provide a short plain-English privacy note accurately explaining how data is processed and whether it is stored or sent to a third party.
- [x] Tone: warm, calm, non-judgmental, 8th-grade reading level, no scary language, no shaming about weight. Wellness framing only: NEVER diagnose, NEVER name a disease as a conclusion, NEVER recommend or adjust medication or doses. Use phrases like “your numbers are in a range that is often linked with...” and “worth discussing with a doctor”.
- [x] Rename all visible app branding from VitalCheck to **Health Tracker**, including the page title, header and footer, privacy/support screens, result text, PDF heading, download filename, README and documentation; retain the private-local-only/no-AI wording.
- [x] Style the interface with Tailwind utility classes and Ant Design dropdowns; preserve labels, keyboard access, descriptions, validation state, visible focus, dark-mode styling and readable touch targets. Keep restrained Framer Motion transitions and respect reduced-motion settings.
- [x] English is the default and only locale. An Urdu toggle with correct RTL was optional in the original brief and was not added.

## 3. Deterministic categories, score and interpretations
- [x] The LLM must never calculate or classify health values. The approved app uses no LLM; every number and category comes from deterministic code.
- [x] Blood pressure — keep AHA categories: Normal <120 AND <80; Elevated 120–129 AND <80; Stage 1 130–139 OR 80–89; Stage 2 >=140 OR >=90. If the readings differ, use the higher category. For a reading >180 systolic or >120 diastolic, tell the user to wait at least one minute and repeat. If it remains similarly high without concerning symptoms, say to contact a healthcare professional as soon as possible; do not tell an asymptomatic person to call an ambulance from one number alone. If very high pressure is accompanied by chest pain, shortness of breath, back pain, numbness, weakness, new vision change, or difficulty speaking, show emergency instructions to call the local emergency number now. Keep the low-BP-below-90/60 note non-diagnostic, especially when dizziness/fainting/fatigue is reported. Explain that one reading is not a diagnosis; use a validated cuff and calm repeat readings.
- [x] BMI = kg / m². Standard WHO: <18.5 underweight, 18.5–24.9 normal, 25–29.9 overweight, >=30 obese. South/East Asian background uses Asia-Pacific cutoffs (23 increased risk, 27.5 high risk; 18.5 lower bound unchanged). Always caveat that BMI can mislead for athletes/very muscular people, older adults who lost muscle, and pregnancy. If waist supplied, compute waist-to-height ratio; >=0.5 is higher risk and show as the better signal.
- [x] Sleep: preserve the broad adult <6h short; 6–7 borderline; 7–9 recommended range; >9 long reference, but state that sleep needs and older-adult public guidance vary and use this as a soft guide rather than a medical finding.
- [x] Resting heart rate: flag <50 or >100 with context; athletes can have a low rate and should not be alarmed.
- [x] Energy/Fatigue Score 0–100, higher = more energy, transparent weighted model. Store every weight in one config file for tuning. Consider sleep duration & quality (largest weight), activity/sedentary time, stress/mood, BP outside normal/resting HR, BMI/waist category, hydration/caffeine, and symptom count. Show score, band (High energy / Moderate / Low / Very low), top 3 factors dragging it down, each with the user’s own number. Label “A lifestyle-based estimate, not a medical measurement.”
- [x] Fasting laboratory glucose context (non-pregnant adults only): <100 mg/dL (<5.6 mmol/L) is below the usual screening threshold; 100–125 mg/dL (5.6–6.9 mmol/L) is within a commonly used increased-risk screening range; >=126 mg/dL (>=7.0 mmol/L) is at/above a commonly used diabetes screening threshold. HbA1c: <5.7% below the usual threshold; 5.7–6.4% within the increased-risk screening range; >=6.5% at/above a commonly used diabetes screening threshold. Use “screening range/threshold” wording only; never conclude the user has a disease. Explain that a clinician confirms results and may use another test. Do not classify during pregnancy, for known diabetes/personalized targets, for home-meter/other/uncertain readings, or for HbA1c when anemia is reported. The glucose field must not silently change the lifestyle energy score.
- [x] Same inputs yield the same score/categories. No invented numbers or facts.

## 5. Emergency and population-specific handling
- [x] A single reading >180 systolic and/or >120 diastolic does not automatically tell an asymptomatic user to call an ambulance. If listed concerning symptoms are present with the very high reading, show the emergency instruction before the normal report: “Call your local emergency number now.” If those symptoms are absent, say to wait at least one minute and repeat; if the second reading is similarly high, contact a healthcare professional as soon as possible. Do not show cheerful tips before this safety message.
- [x] Chest discomfort + shortness of breath, fainting, or palpitations with dizziness => urgent advice regardless of score.
- [x] Any mention in free text of self-harm, wanting to die, or severe depression => stop analysis; respond supportively and encourage contacting local emergency/crisis services or a trusted person. Never ignore.
- [x] Pregnancy with high BP, headache, vision changes, or swelling => urgent advice.
- [x] Pregnant/breastfeeding: skip BMI category and weight advice; use pregnancy-specific message.
- [x] Age 65+: do not push weight loss for slightly high BMI; low BMI and unintentional weight loss are bigger concern; treat low BP + dizziness (fall risk) carefully.
- [x] Athletes/very muscular: checkbox softens BMI interpretation; low resting HR is not alarming.
- [x] Asian/South Asian background: use adjusted BMI cutoffs; emphasize waist measure.
- [x] Known diabetes/heart/kidney/thyroid conditions or medications: personalized targets belong to their doctor; show “your doctor’s targets may differ” and avoid overriding.
- [x] Medication yes: add note beta-blockers/BP meds can change heart rate and BP.
- [x] Shift workers/new parents: sleep may be irregular by necessity; avoid blame and offer realistic tips.
- [x] Underweight + fatigue: do NOT suggest weight loss. If free text suggests disordered eating, respond with care and no numeric diet targets.
- [x] Conflicting inputs (e.g. “7 hours sleep” but “wake unrefreshed + snoring”): mention possible poor-quality sleep and suggest discussing with a doctor; do not name sleep apnea as a diagnosis.
- [x] Crisis, red-flag and pregnancy emergency rules override score, ordinary action tips, and reassuring summary.
